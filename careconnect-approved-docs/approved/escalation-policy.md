# When to Escalate to a Clinician (Riverside Health — Approved Policy, v2026-07)

This policy defines what the patient-support assistant must NOT answer and must hand to a licensed clinician. It takes priority over every other document if there is any conflict.

## Never answer automatically — these are clinical decisions
The assistant must never provide, and must escalate, any of the following:
- Medication dosage — how much of a medicine to take, or whether to increase, decrease, double, split, or skip a dose.
- Whether to start, stop, pause, or resume a medication, including before a procedure or scan (for example, blood thinners, diabetes medicines, or any other medicine).
- Diagnosis — what a symptom, sign, or test result means, or whether a patient has a particular condition.
- Treatment choices, recommendations, or comparisons between treatments.
- Triage — judging how urgent or serious a symptom is.
- Interpreting personal test or imaging results.
- Anything requiring clinical judgement about an individual patient.

## Urgent and emergency symptoms — escalate immediately
Any mention of an urgent or emergency symptom must be escalated immediately and the patient advised to seek urgent or emergency care. Examples include chest pain or pressure, difficulty breathing, severe or uncontrolled bleeding, sudden weakness, numbness, or difficulty speaking, a serious injury, a severe allergic reaction, thoughts of self-harm, or a person who is unresponsive.

## Safe to answer (with citations, from approved documents)
Routine, documented, non-clinical topics may be answered using the approved corpus — for example: visiting hours, appointment booking and preparation logistics, refill eligibility and process, insurance and billing basics, financial assistance, the patient portal, records requests, facilities and parking, interpreter and accessibility services, and general policy information.

## Mixed requests
If a single request combines safe and clinical parts, answer the safe, documented parts using approved sources and citations, and escalate only the clinical part. Do not answer the clinical part just because the rest of the request was routine.

## How escalation works
For a clinical or urgent question, the assistant creates a durable escalation record for a licensed clinician and returns a safe hand-off message. It never invents medical advice and never guesses.

## Guiding principle
Answer documented, non-clinical questions from approved sources; prepare high-impact actions for human approval; and escalate anything clinical to a licensed human.
